Tokyo room prototype
- Click Tokyo, then Explore. Only the room and “Hover over objects to explore” are visible. Click/tap the diary for Japanese Blog, books for Japanese Books, the record player for the supplied song, either curtain to open/close both panels, or the door to return to the globe. Hidden semantic buttons retain keyboard/screen-reader access and outline their corresponding object on focus. Escape closes content or leaves the room.
- assets/JapanRoom.glb is a web export of the updated JapanRoom.blend, made from a temporary copy without changing the supplied Blender file. tools/export-japan-room.py freezes the evaluated curtain at the authored frame (215), then bakes the original UV shader graphs into color, packed roughness/metalness and tangent normal textures. This preserves Poliigon tiling, color/AO mixing and normal strength, including the 3× wall/ceiling scale. The source bake uses 2048px wall textures and 1024px other textures. tools/optimize-room-assets.py then packs WebP base colors without changing their resolution or UVs, and caps normal/ORM maps to sizes appropriate for the fixed room view (512px; 1024px for large normals). Geometry and UV data are copied unchanged. The Cycles glass mix becomes glTF transmission. Bake caches are keyed by the source file hash; the script never saves a .blend.
- The photographic export adds millimetre-scale bevels with weighted normals and slight book/diary placement differences. tools/light-japan-room.py bakes 256-sample Cycles indirect diffuse lighting into two separate 2048px HDR atlases: assets/room-indirect.hdr for architecture and assets/room-furniture.hdr for furniture. A second UV set provides unique lightmap coordinates with explicit island padding; the original material UVs and texture scale are preserved. Material UV0 arrays are checked unchanged after the atlas bake. Fine normal maps are excluded from the lighting bake to avoid undersampling blotches, then restored for live shading. For the fixed evening light, tools/bake-room-joinery.py rebakes architecture and the two large desk/bookcase meshes at 1024 samples. The joinery receives its own padded 2048px atlas; triangle-corner UV1 sidecars leave positions, normals and material UV0 exactly unchanged. HDR denoising replaces the old blurred furniture lighting on these surfaces. The HDR atlases are packed into linear RGBM PNGs with range 32; the runtime decodes them, converts diffuse radiance to irradiance and adds shadowed direct sunlight. This reduces download and GPU storage without clipping the bake. Wall wear follows the corner, skirting and window sill rather than random patches.
- The linen uses opaque depth testing, thin-fiber backlighting and sheen, subtle hem shading and partially transmitting shadows. It deliberately avoids screen-space refraction: overlapping folds sampled one another and crawled with the camera. Flat window glass uses Fresnel reflection in one pass, keeping the exterior sharp without a separate refraction buffer. A breeze displaces the lower folds by at most about 1.3cm while the header stays pinned; reduced motion freezes it, and hidden rooms stop updating it. This small flutter uses cached rest-pose shadows and diffuse lighting. Dreamlike air combines neutral distance haze (density 0.025), shadowed window shafts modulated by drifting fog, and slowly drifting green/pink pastel banks in the lower room. Dense upper-window scattering is avoided so opaque curtain folds remain clear. Full-resolution opaque depth and depth-aware upsampling keep the atmosphere clipped to furniture and both sides of the curtains. A single 64-point draw adds sparse dust motes inside the window-light volume. The final compositor diffuses highlights and adds a gentle edge vignette while retaining surface detail. Reduced motion freezes dust and mist; hidden rooms stop their clocks. Contact shading preserves furniture depth.
- The window uses Nelemson G’s fixed Tokyo skyline recording “Aerial View of Tokyo Skyline at Dusk” under the Pexels License. The silent 2560×1440 local loop plays at native speed (playbackRate 1); alternate source frames are dropped from 60fps to 30fps without retiming. A one-second dissolve joins the loop. The room stays in a warm pre-twilight setting regardless of the real clock, with matching low-angle direct sunlight and baked bounce. Playback pauses outside the room or on hidden tabs; reduced motion and failed decoding use a matching JPEG. Source and edit details are in assets/tokyo-window.credit.md.
- tokyo-exterior.js positions one distant city plane along the actual window sightline, aligned with the initial camera to avoid oblique stretching. Its smaller angular scale gives the city a more believable distance, preserving the footage aspect and showing more of its natural skyline. A continuous blue evening-sky gradient replaces stretched edge pixels above the film, with a soft horizon blend and warm color adaptation. Soft banks of fog move very slightly across the city, becoming denser near the distant skyline. Their clock stops outside the room and with reduced motion; the video keeps its native speed. During SPIN, a song-driven blend warms the same city image and room sunlight/sky fill and adds a rolling dust veil plus two depths of fast antialiased sand trails. The storm is rendered only on the exterior plane, naturally hidden by the apartment geometry. It preserves the city geometry and keeps the fully buffered 27-second footage looping for the entire song. Dust uses the room clock and continues smoothly across clip loops. No second video or additional artwork is loaded. Pause/end/hidden/exit restore the ordinary light. The authored window, glass, sill and curtains provide the foreground; no invented buildings, rails or props are added. Small camera translation creates parallax against the distant image. The skyline remains recorded image-based scenery, not a navigable 3D city.
- Room contents must remain authored by the user. Do not add lamps, books, records, decorations or other props in code; edit existing objects and suggest any additions for the user to make. The runtime retains dark-wood reflectance, vinyl microgroove shading, original furniture/door bevels and subtle hem irregularity without introducing extra room contents.

- The user-provided SBRPoster OBJ retains its original placement, curved mesh and image UV crop. assets/SBR.webp supplies the artwork; a satin paper physical material receives live sunlight and the local reflection probe. Separate UV1 coordinates project the wall's indirect illumination onto the paper without changing its image UV0.
- The record control pauses/resumes the song. Each start has a 2.5-second vinyl lead-in before the original song opening or saved pause position, with a brief music fade-in. Soft generated groove noise and irregular ticks continue quietly beneath the music. Cancelling during the lead-in, leaving the room or hiding the tab clears the timer and stops the surface bed. After the track ends naturally, a 2.2-second generated vinyl runout/crackle plays, followed by a pickup click; the record stops and its control resets. Leaving the room or hiding the tab cancels both the song and its ending sound. The supplied Kroi — SPIN M4A is copied unchanged to assets/kroi-spin.m4a. Its supplied artwork, assets/kroi-spin-cover.png, is shared by the circular vinyl label and the shelf sleeve.
- The export stores roomAction metadata on the books, door, diary and record-player nodes, including multi-material groups. The desk and speakers stay noninteractive. The authored camera gets a small local left/backward offset and a 12% wider lens, with gentle mouse movement and responsive framing. The vinyl rotates while music plays. Material roughness floors distinguish matte plaster/fabric, satin wood, vinyl and metal; a static 128px interior reflection probe and all post effects warm up before flight. Dark wood keeps its authored albedo and grain with restrained rough reflections; curtain transmission and light shafts share the sun’s warm color.
- Hover and click picking accept a ten-CSS-pixel margin around visible object silhouettes, with nearby rays retaining occlusion checks. Hover contours use the occlusion-aware Three.js OutlinePass mask with byte-packed depth and a solid, antialiased 4px white stroke around the selected mesh union. The overlay uses normal blending after the final dream-glow compositor, so the glow cannot produce offset copies of the white contour. It preserves object materials; both speakers are excluded from the record-player interaction.
- tools/prepare-flight-assets.cjs prepares fifteen local WebP imagery atlases for tokyo-flight.js on the existing Three.js globe: NASA / GIBS natural-color Blue Marble for the country view, then GSI seamlessphoto for regional and city detail. The globe and imagery share night lighting and one reversible lighting curve, avoiding a night-to-day swap or vivid ocean-depth colors during the handoff. Wider atlases and pixel-ratio-aware overlap replace the orbit texture before it gets magnified; only useful neighboring levels render during the flight. One camera follows the same continuous route from orbit to Tokyo rooftops and back. Imagery credits are retained in the Towski.dev project drawer and asset credit files; flights have no visible captions or credit overlays. The local atlases avoid hundreds of third-party tile requests on every visit; source credits are in assets/tokyo-flight/credit.md. Label-free elevation/coast masks replace the differently exposed ocean patches with continuous water. No API key is needed. An imagery timeout falls back to the existing globe; reduced motion uses a short fade.
- Each normal arrival/departure lasts 4.8 seconds, including the cloud and white fades. Arrival uses stronger easing with flat velocity, acceleration, and jerk at either end; departure spends about 1.2 seconds fading the room into white before the pullback. Imagery, shaders, textures, and the static room shadow are prepared before the timed flight; a connected volumetric cloud field sits above the Tokyo approach from the start, with uneven banks, clear openings and wispy edges that fade out. The field extends beyond the close city view, drifts slowly and grows through camera perspective. A reversible distance fade reveals it only as the city fills the view, keeping the outer perimeter out of sight. Its density and sunlight are generated once offline and loaded as compressed data; the renderer traces their depth instead of repeating a flat image. Cloud proximity triggers the white fade before the full-white scene handoff, without expensive blur filters. Flight mist builds before arrival and clears during departure; the room uses neutral distance haze, low drifting mist, dust motes and soft highlight diffusion.
- Room textures upload in short batches, reflections capture one face per frame, and material shaders compile against the same linear render target used in the room. GPU completion uses an asynchronous fence. The resting globe renders at 30fps; interaction, flights and the room keep the full animation rate. Wordmark layout updates only when its inputs change.

Rebuilding the photographic room
- Copy the updated source .blend into a temporary directory; never run preparation on the original. Keep object and collection names for the existing interactions.
- From the repository run: `Blender --background --disable-autoexec /tmp/JapanRoom-copy.blend --python tools/export-japan-room.py -- /tmp/JapanRoom.glb --photographic` (use the installed Blender executable path).
- Keep the three matching raw outputs together: `JapanRoom.glb`, `room-indirect.hdr`, and `room-furniture.hdr` into `assets/`, then update their version query in japan-room.js. Before deployment run `python tools/optimize-room-assets.py /tmp/JapanRoom.glb assets/JapanRoom.glb` with Pillow/NumPy available and the two raw HDR files beside the destination. Deploy the optimized GLB. Retain the active room-furniture.png bake under art/lightmaps and run tools/pack-lightmaps.py to create pixel-identical runtime WebP. The raw HDRs are rebuild inputs, not runtime downloads. The default export without `--photographic` is only for unlit material inspection.
- After optimizing the export, run `Blender --background --factory-startup --python tools/bake-room-joinery.py -- /tmp/JapanRoom.glb /tmp/room-evening`. Use the matching raw GLB as input; image optimization does not change geometry. Copy `room-evening-architecture.png`, `room-joinery.png`, `room-joinery-uv.json` and `room-joinery-uv.bin` from that output directory to assets together, then update their runtime version queries. Rebuild these whenever the room geometry changes. The script uses a disposable Blender scene and never opens or saves the source .blend.
- The bake preserves the original camera aspect after denoising and the evaluated curtain pose. Material bake caches include the source SHA256; lighting is freshly baked for each photographic export. Download/preparation failures dispose of loaded textures and media before allowing a retry.

Run `node tests/japan-room-realism.browser.cjs` to check physical parallax, horizon clipping, stationary reduced-motion framing, authored props only, exact material UV0/normal/position preservation and bounded draw calls.

- Curtain interaction spreads the existing simulated folds across their rod, with a 6mm contact gap. Each original fabric row unfolds into its own half of the window, and contact constraints keep the panels separate even during the breeze. Folds flatten slightly while spreading instead of cutting through the opposite panel. Clicking either panel or its keyboard control reverses the motion. Reduced motion applies the new position immediately. Closing dims the baked bounce, direct sunlight and fabric backlight, suppresses shafts/dust, and updates live curtain shadows. Reopening restores the original view. Run `node tests/japan-room-curtains.browser.cjs` for click/keyboard and cover/reopen checks, and `node tests/tokyo-exterior-fog.browser.cjs` for subtle fog motion and freeze behavior. The outline test also checks the final composited display.

- Arrival/departure contain no visible bottom captions, coordinates, credits or room hints. Planet marker dots and halos fade out at departure from orbit and fade back in across the final return to orbit, using the same pulse under a smooth opacity envelope. Markers also fade near the planet limb. Navigation and the wordmark fade back in after the flight. Run `node tests/tokyo-transition-polish.browser.cjs` to verify absence of visible text, continuous camera/scale handoff and bounded frame-to-frame marker changes. Final visual review — 2026-10-21
- Cover artwork appears only on the exterior faces of each front/back board. Interior faces use matte uncoated lining; existing bevels retain their edge material. `node tests/book-cover-lining.browser.cjs` checks both faces of all three novels and captures the opening pose.
- Mobile book exchanges have an extra 4cm of rear clearance. The existing motion check samples every pickup, exchange and return at 120Hz on desktop and phone layouts, including interrupted motion and resize.
- Phones rotated sideways use a proportioned spread with compact headings and vocabulary so navigation and a usable scrolling reading area stay on screen. Run `node tests/book-reader-landscape.browser.cjs`; motion checks also cover the 667×375 landscape layout and wait for the resized camera before sampling.
- Lower pastel mist is slightly thinner and highlight diffusion starts at a brighter threshold, preserving the atmosphere while giving furniture more definition. Atmospheric color is filtered and composited with premultiplied alpha, avoiding bright low-alpha clipping and edge contamination. Content drawers use darker, softer glass so background lettering does not compete with their text.
- Motion and transparency preferences now update during a visit. Video and mist freeze/resume with Reduce Motion; changing it during a flight preserves the current normalized progress. `node tests/site-polish.browser.cjs` exercises live preference changes and simulates a device without floating-point render targets. That fallback uses supported byte buffers throughout SSAO, fog, bloom and contours, retains baked/sky illumination, skips the unsupported reflection capture, and disposes cleanly.
- Legacy diary checks now inspect the persistent visible binding rather than the hidden source model or removed temporary replacement. Isolated geometry checks wait for startup to finish before loading their fixture, and audio checks wait for the first audio rendering quantum before reading scheduled gain.
